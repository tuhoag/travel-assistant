# Berlin

## Overview

Berlin is the capital and largest city of Germany, situated on the Spree River in the northeastern part of the country. First documented in the 13th century, around 1237, the city grew from a medieval trading settlement into the capital of Prussia, the German Empire, the Weimar Republic, and Nazi Germany, before being divided between East and West during the Cold War. Since German reunification in 1990, Berlin has again served as the capital of a unified Germany. The city covers about 891 square kilometers and has a population of approximately 3.7 million people.

Berlin is one of Germany's sixteen federal states as well as its capital city, giving it a dual status similar to other city-states such as Hamburg and Bremen. It is governed by a mayor and a state parliament, the Abgeordnetenhaus, which sits in the historic Prussian parliament building. The metropolitan region surrounding the city, often referred to as Berlin-Brandenburg, extends well beyond the city limits and is home to more than 6 million people.

The city's layout still bears the marks of its Cold War division. The former path of the Berlin Wall, which separated East and West Berlin from 1961 to 1989, can still be traced through the city, with a preserved section known as the East Side Gallery now serving as an open-air memorial covered in murals painted by artists from around the world.

## Government and Culture

Berlin is Germany's political and cultural center, home to the Bundestag, the Chancellery, and the official residence of the President at Schloss Bellevue. The city is known for landmarks that reflect its turbulent history, including the Brandenburg Gate, the Berlin Wall Memorial, and the Reichstag building, as well as a vibrant contemporary arts and nightlife scene that has made it one of Europe's most dynamic cultural capitals.

Beyond politics, Berlin has become one of Europe's leading centers for startups, technology, and creative industries, attracting entrepreneurs and artists from across the globe. Its relatively low cost of living compared to other Western European capitals, combined with a permissive cultural atmosphere, helped fuel a wave of growth in its tech and design sectors during the 2010s and 2020s.

The city also hosts a dense concentration of museums and cultural institutions, most notably the Museum Island complex on the Spree, a UNESCO World Heritage Site containing five major museums. Berlin's opera houses, philharmonic orchestra, and hundreds of art galleries further reinforce its reputation as one of the world's foremost centers for the arts.
