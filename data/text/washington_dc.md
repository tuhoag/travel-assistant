# Washington, D.C.

## Overview

Washington, D.C., formally the District of Columbia and commonly known as Washington or D.C., is the capital city of the United States. Founded in 1790, it sits on the east bank of the Potomac River, bordering Maryland and Virginia, and was established as the seat of the federal government following the passage of the Residence Act. The city covers an area of 177 square kilometers and has a population of roughly 690,000 residents, with more than 6.3 million people living in the surrounding metropolitan area.

The district was carved out of land ceded by Maryland and Virginia, and its layout was originally designed by French-born engineer Pierre Charles L'Enfant, who envisioned broad diagonal avenues radiating from the Capitol and the White House. Because it is a federal district rather than a state, Washington, D.C. has a unique political status: its residents did not gain the right to vote for president until the 23rd Amendment in 1961, and the city still lacks full voting representation in Congress.

Unlike most national capitals, Washington, D.C. was purpose-built to serve as a seat of government rather than growing organically from an existing settlement, a distinction it shares with cities such as Canberra and Brasília.

## Government and Landmarks

As the political heart of the country, Washington, D.C. houses all three branches of the U.S. federal government, including the White House, the U.S. Capitol, and the Supreme Court. It is also renowned for its extensive collection of national monuments and museums, many of which are administered by the Smithsonian Institution and located along or near the National Mall. Beyond government, the city has become a hub for international diplomacy, hosting numerous embassies and the headquarters of organizations such as the World Bank and the Organization of American States.

The National Mall stretches from the Lincoln Memorial to the U.S. Capitol and is lined with iconic monuments, including the Washington Monument, the Jefferson Memorial, and the Vietnam Veterans Memorial. Most of the Smithsonian museums along the Mall, such as the National Air and Space Museum and the National Museum of Natural History, offer free admission, making the city one of the most accessible cultural destinations in the country.

Washington, D.C. is also home to a significant local population and neighborhoods with their own distinct character, including Georgetown, Adams Morgan, and Capitol Hill. Despite being dominated by federal institutions, the city has a growing economy in technology, education, and biotechnology, anchored by universities such as Georgetown and George Washington University.
