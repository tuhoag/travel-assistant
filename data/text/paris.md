# Paris

## Overview

Paris is the capital and most populous city of France, situated along the Seine River in the north-central part of the country. Its origins trace back to around the 3rd century BC, when a Celtic tribe known as the Parisii settled on the Île de la Cité, in what the Romans later called Lutetia. The city proper covers about 105 square kilometers and has a population of roughly 2.1 million, while its wider metropolitan area is home to around 11 million people.

The city proper is divided into 20 administrative districts known as arrondissements, arranged in a clockwise spiral starting from the center near the Louvre. This compact layout, combined with strict height restrictions on buildings, has helped preserve much of the historic low-rise character of central Paris even as the surrounding metropolitan area has grown substantially.

Much of the city's present appearance dates to the mid-19th century, when Baron Georges-Eugène Haussmann undertook a massive renovation project under Napoleon III, demolishing narrow medieval streets in favor of wide boulevards, uniform building facades, and expansive public parks.

## Culture and Landmarks

Known as the "City of Light," Paris has long been a global center for art, fashion, gastronomy, and culture, and it remains one of the most visited cities in the world. It is home to iconic landmarks such as the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre Museum, Notre-Dame Cathedral, and the Arc de Triomphe, as well as a network of grand boulevards shaped by the 19th-century renovations of Baron Haussmann. As the political and economic capital of France, Paris also hosts the country's national government and numerous international organizations, including UNESCO.

The city's association with fashion dates back centuries and continues today through Paris Fashion Week, one of the "Big Four" fashion weeks alongside New York, London, and Milan. Paris is similarly renowned for its culinary tradition, home to numerous Michelin-starred restaurants as well as a deep café culture that has shaped French social life for generations.

Paris regularly ranks among the most visited cities on Earth, drawing tens of millions of tourists annually to sites such as the Musée d'Orsay, the Sacré-Cœur Basilica, and the Palace of Versailles just outside the city. The Seine River itself, along with its historic bridges and quays, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991.
