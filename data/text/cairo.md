# Cairo

## Overview

Cairo is the capital of Egypt and the largest city in the Arab world, located near the Nile River Delta in the northeastern part of the country. The city was founded in 969 AD by the Fatimid dynasty, though the surrounding region had been a center of settlement for millennia, including the nearby ancient city of Memphis and the Giza pyramid complex. Greater Cairo covers an area of roughly 3,085 square kilometers and has a population of about 10 million within the city itself, with more than 20 million people living in the wider metropolitan area.

Cairo's location near the apex of the Nile Delta has made it a strategic center of trade and governance for thousands of years, long before the city itself was formally founded. The historic Islamic core of the city, with its dense network of mosques, markets, and medieval gates, remains largely intact and is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The city's name in Arabic, Al-Qahirah, is often translated as "the Victorious," reflecting the circumstances of its founding by the Fatimid general Jawhar al-Siqilli. Over subsequent centuries, Cairo grew into one of the most important cities in the Islamic world, particularly during the Mamluk period.

## Culture and Economy

As Egypt's political, economic, and cultural capital, Cairo hosts the country's government institutions and is often referred to as the "City of a Thousand Minarets" for its rich collection of Islamic architecture. The city is also home to the Egyptian Museum, home to a vast collection of pharaonic antiquities, and serves as a major hub for media, education, and commerce throughout the Middle East and North Africa.

Cairo's film and television industry has historically made it the entertainment capital of the Arabic-speaking world, producing a large share of the region's movies and soap operas since the early 20th century. The city is also home to Al-Azhar University, founded in 970 AD, one of the oldest continuously operating universities in the world and a leading center of Islamic learning.

Just outside the city proper lie the Pyramids of Giza and the Sphinx, among the most famous monuments on Earth, drawing millions of tourists to the Cairo metropolitan area each year despite periods of political instability that have affected the tourism industry. In recent years, the Egyptian government has developed a new administrative capital east of Cairo intended to relieve pressure on the existing city's infrastructure.
