# Canberra

## Overview

Canberra is the capital city of Australia, purpose-built as a compromise between the rival cities of Sydney and Melbourne, both of which sought to be the nation's capital following federation in 1901. Founded in 1913 and designed by American architects Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin, the city is located within the Australian Capital Territory, roughly 280 kilometers southwest of Sydney. Canberra covers an area of about 814 square kilometers and has a population of approximately 460,000 people.

The name Canberra is believed to derive from an Aboriginal word meaning "meeting place," reflecting the area's long history as a gathering site for the Ngunnawal people prior to European settlement. The Griffins' original plan envisioned a garden city built around geometric patterns and sightlines connecting major landmarks, a design still evident in the city's layout today.

Because it was built specifically to serve as the national capital, Canberra lacked much of the infrastructure of an established city in its early decades, and its population grew slowly until after World War II, when the expansion of the federal government accelerated the city's development.

## Government and Design

As the seat of the Australian federal government, Canberra is home to Parliament House, the High Court of Australia, and numerous national institutions, including the Australian War Memorial and the National Gallery of Australia. Unlike many national capitals, the city was carefully planned around Lake Burley Griffin and extensive parklands, giving it a distinctive garden-city character.

Parliament House, opened in 1988, is built into a hill and topped with a large flagpole, symbolically placing the legislature above the old provisional parliament building it replaced. The surrounding Parliamentary Triangle contains many of the city's most significant national institutions, connected by ceremonial boulevards.

Canberra consistently ranks highly in global livability surveys, owing in part to its low population density, extensive green space, and high proportion of public servants and university-educated residents. The Australian National University, located in the city, is among the country's leading research institutions.
