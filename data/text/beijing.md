# Beijing

## Overview

Beijing is the capital of the People's Republic of China and one of the most populous cities in the world. Its history as a settlement stretches back more than three thousand years, and it has served as the capital of various Chinese dynasties and states for much of the past eight centuries, most notably during the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties. The municipality covers a large administrative area of about 16,410 square kilometers and has a population of roughly 21.5 million people.

Beijing is one of four municipalities in China that are directly administered by the central government, giving it a status roughly equivalent to a province. Its urban core is organized around a series of concentric ring roads, with the historic center, once enclosed by city walls that have since largely been demolished, still forming the political and symbolic heart of the city.

The city's name has changed several times throughout history, having been known as Yanjing, Zhongdu, Dadu, and Beiping at various points before settling on Beijing, meaning "Northern Capital," which it has held for most of the period since the Ming dynasty.

## Government and Landmarks

As China's political and cultural center, Beijing houses the country's national government, including the Communist Party leadership and the State Council. The city is renowned for its historic landmarks, such as the Forbidden City, Tiananmen Square, and the Temple of Heaven, as well as nearby sections of the Great Wall of China. Beijing is also a major center for education, research, and international diplomacy, home to leading universities and numerous foreign embassies.

The Forbidden City, a vast imperial palace complex at the heart of Beijing, served as the residence of Chinese emperors for nearly five centuries and is now one of the most visited museums in the world. Tiananmen Square, immediately to its south, is among the largest public squares on Earth and has been the site of numerous major events in modern Chinese history.

Beijing hosted the Summer Olympics in 2008 and the Winter Olympics in 2022, making it the first city to host both. The city is also home to two of China's most prestigious universities, Peking University and Tsinghua University, which anchor a growing technology and research sector in the northwestern Haidian district.
