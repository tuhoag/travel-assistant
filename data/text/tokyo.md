# Tokyo

## Overview

Tokyo is the capital of Japan and one of the most populous metropolitan areas in the world. It began as a small fishing village called Edo, which grew into a major castle town after Tokugawa Ieyasu established his shogunate there in 1457. The city was renamed Tokyo, meaning "Eastern Capital," when it became the imperial capital in 1868. Today, the Tokyo Metropolis covers about 2,194 square kilometers, is home to roughly 14 million residents, and anchors a greater metropolitan area of about 37 million people, the largest in the world.

Tokyo is technically not a single city but a metropolitan prefecture made up of 23 special wards, each functioning with a degree of autonomy similar to an individual city, along with numerous smaller cities, towns, and villages further west, including the Izu and Ogasawara island chains far out in the Pacific Ocean.

Despite its immense size, Tokyo has repeatedly rebuilt itself after catastrophic events, including the Great Kanto earthquake of 1923 and extensive firebombing during World War II. Its rapid postwar reconstruction culminated in hosting the 1964 Summer Olympics, a symbolic moment marking Japan's reemergence as a major global economy.

## Economy and Landmarks

Located on the eastern coast of the island of Honshu, Tokyo serves as Japan's political, economic, and cultural center. The city is known for blending cutting-edge technology and towering skyscrapers with historic sites such as the Imperial Palace and the Senso-ji temple in Asakusa. It is also a global hub for business and innovation, hosting the Tokyo Stock Exchange and the headquarters of numerous multinational corporations.

Tokyo's metropolitan economy is among the largest of any city in the world, comparable in size to that of entire mid-sized countries. The city is a major center for finance, electronics, and media, and is consistently ranked among the world's most livable and safest major cities despite its extremely high population density.

The city's transportation network is famed for its efficiency, anchored by Shinjuku Station, the busiest railway station in the world by passenger volume. Neighborhoods such as Shibuya, Akihabara, and Ginza each offer distinct character, from youth fashion culture to electronics retail to upscale shopping, reflecting the diversity contained within Tokyo's sprawling urban fabric.
