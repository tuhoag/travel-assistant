# London

## Overview

London is the capital and largest city of both England and the United Kingdom. It stands on the River Thames in southeastern England and has been a major settlement for more than two thousand years, tracing its origins to the Roman town of Londinium, founded around 47 AD. Today, Greater London spans approximately 1,572 square kilometers and is home to about 8.9 million people, making it one of the most populous cities in Europe.

London is administratively divided into 32 boroughs plus the historic City of London, a small area at its core that retains its own ancient governance structure dating back centuries. The wider metropolitan area, sometimes called the London commuter belt, extends well beyond the boundaries of Greater London and supports a population of over 14 million people.

The city has grown through successive waves of expansion, from the walled Roman and medieval city to the sprawling metropolis shaped by 19th-century industrialization and the construction of its extensive railway and Underground network, the oldest metro system in the world, which opened in 1863.

## Culture and Landmarks

Over the centuries, London has grown from a Roman trading post into one of the world's leading global cities, with major influence in finance, the arts, education, and politics. It is the seat of the UK Parliament and the residence of the British monarch at Buckingham Palace. The city's historic and cultural landmarks, including the Tower of London, Westminster Abbey, and the British Museum, draw millions of visitors each year, while the City of London remains one of the largest financial centers in the world.

London is also one of the most linguistically and culturally diverse cities in the world, with residents speaking hundreds of languages and a significant proportion of the population born outside the United Kingdom. This diversity is reflected in neighborhoods such as Brick Lane, Chinatown, and Southall, each associated with distinct immigrant communities.

The city's cultural institutions extend well beyond its famous museums, encompassing world-renowned theaters in the West End, major music venues, and a thriving contemporary art scene centered around areas like Shoreditch and the South Bank. London has hosted the Summer Olympics three times, more than any other city, most recently in 2012.
