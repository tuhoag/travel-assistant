# Moscow

## Overview

Moscow is the capital and largest city of Russia, located on the Moskva River in the western part of the country. It was first mentioned in historical chronicles in 1147 and grew over the centuries from a small principality into the political and spiritual heart of the Russian state. The city today covers approximately 2,511 square kilometers and has a population of about 13 million people, making it one of the largest cities in Europe.

Moscow's growth has historically radiated outward from the Kremlin in concentric rings, a pattern still visible in the city's road network today. The city serves as the administrative center of Moscow Oblast, though it is itself a separate federal subject of Russia with its own status.

Throughout its history, Moscow has served as the capital of several distinct political entities, including the Grand Duchy of Moscow, the Tsardom of Russia, and the Soviet Union, before becoming the capital of the modern Russian Federation following the dissolution of the USSR in 1991.

## Government and Culture

Moscow serves as the seat of the Russian federal government and is home to the Kremlin, a fortified complex that houses the official residence of the President of Russia, as well as Red Square, one of the country's most iconic public spaces. The city is also Russia's leading center for finance, education, and culture, hosting major institutions such as Moscow State University and the Bolshoi Theatre.

The Moscow Kremlin and Red Square together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site, encompassing landmarks such as Saint Basil's Cathedral, with its distinctive colorful onion domes, and Lenin's Mausoleum. The Kremlin walls enclose numerous cathedrals, palaces, and government buildings that have played central roles in Russian history for centuries.

Moscow is also known for its elaborate metro system, opened in 1935, whose stations are often decorated with elaborate mosaics, chandeliers, and sculptures, earning it a reputation as one of the most architecturally striking public transit systems in the world. The city remains Russia's dominant economic center, generating a disproportionate share of the country's GDP relative to its population.
