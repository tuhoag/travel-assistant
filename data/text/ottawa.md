# Ottawa

## Overview

Ottawa is the capital of Canada, located in the province of Ontario at the confluence of the Ottawa, Rideau, and Gatineau rivers. The settlement was founded in 1826 as Bytown, established to house workers building the Rideau Canal, and was renamed Ottawa in 1855 before being chosen by Queen Victoria as the capital of the Province of Canada in 1857. The city spans approximately 2,790 square kilometers and has a population of roughly 1 million residents within the city proper.

Queen Victoria's choice of Ottawa as the capital was notable for its relative geographic neutrality, situated on the border between English-speaking Canada West and French-speaking Canada East, and for its defensible inland position away from the vulnerable border with the United States.

The Rideau Canal, which runs through the heart of the city, was originally built for military purposes in the early 19th century and today serves as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In winter, a section of the canal is transformed into the world's largest naturally frozen skating rink.

## Government and Region

As the seat of the federal government, Ottawa is home to Parliament Hill, the Supreme Court of Canada, and numerous federal departments and national museums, including the Canadian Museum of History and the National Gallery of Canada. The city sits directly across the Ottawa River from Gatineau, Quebec, and together the two form the National Capital Region, reflecting Canada's bilingual and bicultural heritage.

Parliament Hill, overlooking the Ottawa River, houses the Centre Block, home to the House of Commons and Senate, along with its iconic Peace Tower. The original Centre Block was largely destroyed by fire in 1916 and rebuilt in its current Gothic Revival style, and the building has been undergoing extensive renovation in recent years.

Beyond government, Ottawa has developed a significant technology sector, sometimes referred to as "Silicon Valley North," anchored by companies in telecommunications and software. The city's public service employment base, combined with this growing tech industry, has made it one of the most highly educated metropolitan areas in Canada.
